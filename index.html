The problem

They don't warn you. We do.

The benefits cliff is a specific, devastating moment when years of coverage — Medicaid, nursing care, therapies, feeding supplies, school services — drop away because your child turned a certain age.

At 18, SSI is re-evaluated using adult standards. Katie Beckett closes. At 21, EPSDT ends, GAPP ends, and IDEA's protections expire. The school closes its doors permanently.

Families are rarely told this is coming. The NOW/COMP waiver waitlist averages years, not months. Every year you delay is a year you can't get back.

CLIFF exists to close this gap — translating the bureaucracy, preparing you for interviews, and making sure you know what's coming before it arrives.

The Two Cliffs: What Changes at 18 and at 21

Georgia families of children with significant disabilities face two major benefit transition points — the first at age 18, the second at age 21. Understanding both, and planning ahead for each, is the most important thing you can do right now.

The Age 18 Cliff

Katie Beckett (TEFRA Deeming Waiver) pathway closes. This program allowed children under 18 to qualify for Medicaid regardless of parental income. At 18, the pathway ends — your child must now qualify under adult eligibility categories.

SSI adult redetermination happens. Social Security re-evaluates disability using adult standards — different and often stricter than childhood criteria. Parental income no longer counts — only your child's own income and resources.

Important: Some families who couldn't get SSI as children — because parental income was too high — may now qualify at 18 under adult rules. Apply for SSI at least 3 months before the 18th birthday.

The Age 21 Cliff

EPSDT ends. Adult medical necessity standards apply — stricter and less comprehensive than pediatric coverage.

GAPP ends. In-home skilled nursing and personal care at no cost — ends at 21.

IDEA entitlement ends. All school-based therapies, IEP services, structure, and supervision end simultaneously.

Act Now

The NOW/COMP Planning List has never dropped below 6,000 people. Over 7,300 were waiting as of January 2024. Only 500–513 new slots funded per year. Wait times span multiple years.

Apply to the Planning List while your child is still in school. Every year on the list counts.

Medicaid — Under 18
Katie Beckett (TEFRA Deeming Waiver)

Allows Georgia to ignore parental income when determining Medicaid eligibility for children with disabilities who require institutional-level care but are cared for at home.

Age Limit
18 and younger
Parental Income
Disregarded
Eligibility
Level of care need, not diagnosis
Renewal
Every 2 years minimum

Who Qualifies

  • Child is 18 or younger
  • Does NOT qualify for SSI due to parental income/assets
  • Requires hospital/nursing facility level of care — but is cared for at home
  • Eligibility is NOT diagnosis-based. Requires documented level of care need.

How to Apply

  • Step 1: Apply for SSI first (800-772-1213). If approved, Medicaid comes automatically.
  • Step 2: If SSI denied, keep the denial letter — required for Katie Beckett.
  • Step 3: Contact Katie Beckett Team: 678-248-7449
  • Step 4: Gather ALL medical records from every provider.
  • Step 5: The Level of Care Statement is critical — describe needs fully.
  • Step 6: Do not leave any pages blank. Blank pages cause automatic denial.

The Level of Care Statement makes or breaks applications. Describe what your child cannot do independently, what daily care looks like, and what would happen without it. Do not minimize.

Free Help

Parent to Parent Georgia (p2pga.org) — webinars and guidance. Sheila Carter at CamandMadispromise.org — free application help. DJ Jeyaram — special needs attorney for denials.

In-Home Nursing — Under 21
Georgia Pediatric Program (GAPP)

Georgia's in-home skilled nursing and personal care program for medically fragile children. Your child must already have Georgia Medicaid to access GAPP.

Age Limit
Under 21
Cost
$0 for Medicaid recipients
Nursing Hours
Up to 24 hrs/day
Approval
30–90 days

Who Qualifies

  • Under 21 with active Georgia Medicaid
  • Medically fragile — requires skilled nursing at home
  • Common conditions: ventilator/oxygen dependence, G-tube, complex medical needs

How to Apply

  • Ensure active Georgia Medicaid coverage
  • Select a Medicaid-enrolled GAPP home health agency
  • Agency coordinates authorization with DCH

GAPP ends at 21. Begin planning for NOW/COMP waiver well before. Do not wait for GAPP to end before applying to the Planning List.

Contact: 404-657-7882 | medicaid.georgia.gov

Adult Services — I/DD Waiver
NOW & COMP Waivers

Georgia's primary long-term support programs for adults with intellectual and developmental disabilities. Both administered by DBHDD.

NOW Cap
Up to $25,000/year
COMP Cap
Need-based, no fixed cap
Planning List
7,300+ waiting
Renewal
Annually

NOW vs. COMP

NOW — Support without 24-hour care. Living with family or independently. Cap of $25,000/year.

COMP — Full range of services. Intensive needs or institutional transitions. Higher funding based on need.

Services Covered

  • Supported employment and day support
  • Community access and residential support
  • ABA therapy, OT, behavioral consultation
  • Respite care, transportation, assistive technology
  • Self-direction: hire and manage your own workers

The Planning List is the most critical fact on this page. Over 7,300 waiting. Only 500–513 new slots per year.

Apply NOW — not at 20, not at 21. Every year on the list counts when slots open.

Contact DBHDD: (404) 657-2252 | dbhdd.georgia.gov

Income & Medicaid — Age 18
SSI at Age 18: What Changes

SSI approval equals automatic Georgia Medicaid — the most direct pathway to adult Medicaid.

No longer counted

Two critical facts: (1) Families who couldn't get SSI as children may NOW qualify at 18 under adult rules. (2) Some who had SSI as children may be denied under stricter adult standards. Apply and appeal if denied.

How to Apply

  • Call SSA: (800) 772-1213
  • Apply up to 3 months before 18th birthday
  • If denied, appeal immediately — denials are common

Protecting Eligibility

  • ABLE Account: Tax-advantaged savings for disability expenses (onset before age 26)
  • Special Needs Trust: Holds assets without affecting SSI/Medicaid eligibility
Family Support Services
Bobby Dodd Institute (BDI) & Family Support

BDI is a DBHDD-contracted nonprofit specializing in I/DD services. Their Benefits Navigation program secured 38 waivers in 2023–2024.

The BDI Intake Interview

This is a structured assessment — not a casual conversation. How you present needs directly affects services offered.

The most common mistake: families undersell need.

You've adapted over years. In this interview, describe care at its hardest, and what would happen without support.

What to Stress

  • Daily physical care: feeding, positioning, medications, bathing
  • What happens without care — worst-case scenarios
  • Total caregiver hours and family impact
  • Behavioral needs and safety concerns
  • Medical complexity: diagnoses, specialists, equipment
  • Written documentation from all physicians

Contact BDI: (404) 888-0022 | bobbydodd.org

Education & Transition
School Transition & IDEA Rights

Under IDEA, eligible students are entitled to FAPE through the end of the school year they turn 22 (in Georgia). All IEP services, therapies, and transition planning included.

Transition Planning — Must Begin at 16

The IEP must include transition goals covering employment, independent living, and community participation. Push for specific goals. Document everything in writing.

Critical: Apply to the NOW/COMP Planning List concurrently with school, not after exit. The waitlist averages years.

What to Request

  • Comprehensive transition evaluation (life skills, community, independent living)
  • Specific, measurable post-school goals in the IEP
  • DBHDD Regional Office contact information
  • Referrals to adult service providers before exit
  • Complete documentation of all current services

Our Mission

CLIFF exists to ensure no Georgia family reaches the benefits cliff without knowing what comes next. Access to clear information should not depend on income, education, or connections.

Our Story

CLIFF was born from real conversations with families who spent years fighting for their children — and suddenly found themselves at the edge of a cliff nobody warned them about.

At 18, pathways closed. At 21, everything closed at once. No roadmap, no warning, no call from the state. We founded CLIFF because that experience is preventable.
